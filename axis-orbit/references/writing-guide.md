# Writing guide

Measured from the six published Axis ORbits articles (advita.com/axis/*). Always write in English, even when the user talks in Portuguese.

## Sections

| Section | Length | Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| Title (H1) | 8–14 words | A question or a benefit, in title case. Examples: "Which Patients Are Most at Risk for Glenoid Loosening and Rotator Cuff Tears After aTSA?", "Identifying rTSA Patients Most at Risk for Acromial and Scapular Fracture". |
| Study Question | 1 sentence, 11–18 words, ends with "?" | The clinical question, plain words. |
| The Story | 72–124 words, 1–2 paragraphs | Clinical problem → "<First author>, <senior author> and colleagues analyzed/compared/followed N patients…" → what they set out to do. |
| Key Findings | 4–5 bullets (6 max), 17–35 words each | `**Label:** sentence`. Labels have 2–6 words ("Overall low incidence", "The power of accumulation", "Fractures occur early"). |
| Clinical Context | 38–64 words, 1 paragraph | What a surgeon does differently on Monday. |
| Limitations | 22–32 words | Starts with "These findings are limited by…". |
| Reference | Full citation | Authors, title, journal, year;vol(issue):pages, DOI. For an unpublished manuscript: "Manuscript in preparation; journal and DOI pending." |
| Closing line | Fixed | "The Advita multicenter Axis research group efforts make these generalizable findings possible." |

## Numbers

- Every number in any text field is a `{metric_id}` placeholder. Never type a result number by hand.
- Cut-offs and scale ends that define the study (for example `0.3`, `0.6`, `BMI 30`) go in `definitions`, not in metrics.
- Copy numbers exactly as the source prints them: same decimals, same thousands separators. Do not round, convert or recompute.
- Never derive a ratio, difference, "x times" or "1 in N" from two values. Use only what the paper reports.
- An odds ratio is "higher odds", not "higher risk" or "more likely", unless the paper says so.
- Thousands separator: comma (14,906). Units: %, x, mm, cm³, months.

## Voice

- Plain, confident, clinical. Short sentences. Active voice.
- Use the paper's own terms (aTSA, rTSA, CTA, OA, Equinoxe) and spell out each abbreviation once in The Story.
- No hype: no "groundbreaking", "revolutionary", "game-changer", "unlock", "harness", "delve".
- No em-dash asides, no "not X, but Y", no rhetorical questions outside Study Question.
- Clinical Context gives actions the paper supports. It never overstates causation: retrospective data are "associated with".

## Tags

Pick 1–3 from the site's taxonomy: aTSA Outcomes, rTSA Outcomes, Complications, Technology, GPS Navigation.
